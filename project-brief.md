
## What this project is

A **physical tabletop card and board game**. Two or more players at a table, using printed cards, coins, and a rulebook. No screen, no app, no software.

## What an idea is here

A thing you would make, print, or write down: a card, a rule, a coin, a token, a slot on a board, an icon, a line of rulebook text, or a thing to watch in a playtest.

Not a feeling or a theme statement. "The game should feel tense" is not an idea. "A rule that makes the table turn against a player who is one coin from winning" is an idea.

## What a good question sounds like

It names the specific card, rule, or component, and asks about one concrete decision.

Good: "Does Ministry of Truth remove one UDHR coin from any Institution, or only from one the player controls?"

Bad: "How do you want the theme to come through in the mechanics?" / "How are these concepts represented on the table?"

Every question must name its subject. No "these concepts", no "the mechanics", no inference.

## What to assume

- Physical components only. Printed cards, coins, board, rulebook.
- A rule must be understood from one read. If it needs re-reading, it is a bad rule.
- Playtests are 2–5 people, one evening, no facilitator.
- A card fits roughly 8–15 words of rules text plus a title and a symbol. Longer does not fit.
- Theme is Marxist theory and state capture. Mechanics should let players *use* the ideas, not read about them.

## What NOT to ask about

- 3D models, animation, VR, engines, shaders, code. There is no software.
- Digital tracking, companion apps, online play, single-player.

## What questions should try to settle

Rule clarity. Card text length. Coin economy (scarce enough to hurt, plentiful enough to matter). Runaway leader. Dead turns. Playtest signals — what to watch for that would show a rule is wrong.

## What done looks like

An idea is done when it can be printed or written into the rulebook as-is, a new player can use it after one read, and it does not require the player to hold state the components don't hold.

Do not ask the designer what "done" looks like. Assume the above.
